Every owner complaint filed with NHTSA names the part of the car the owner thinks is at fault. We added up all **665,513 complaints** filed since 2015 for the 902 models on DecodedCars, grouped by that top-level component. The result is a map of where cars most often let their owners down — and a guide to what deserves the closest look on a test drive.

## The top ten

| Component | Complaints | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 131,107 | 19.7% |
| Electrical system | 114,850 | 17.3% |
| Power train (transmission, axles) | 90,411 | 13.6% |
| Unknown or other | 88,169 | 13.2% |
| Air bags | 70,720 | 10.6% |
| Steering | 58,128 | 8.7% |
| Service brakes | 44,632 | 6.7% |
| Fuel / propulsion system | 36,227 | 5.4% |
| Structure | 22,725 | 3.4% |
| Forward collision avoidance | 17,160 | 2.6% |

A single complaint can name more than one component, so the shares add up to more than 100%.

## What stands out

**Engines and electrical systems account for more than a third of everything.** Engine complaints cover stalling, oil consumption, loss of power and failures that strand a car. Electrical complaints are a broad bucket — batteries and charging systems, wiring, warning lights, power windows and, increasingly, infotainment screens and the software behind them.

**The transmission is the third weak point.** "Power train" complaints are dominated by transmissions: harsh or delayed shifts, shuddering, slipping and outright failure. Transmission repairs are among the most expensive a car owner faces outside warranty, which is why a full test drive through every gear matters.

**Air bags rank high, likely helped by recalls.** Many air bag complaints were probably filed during the large inflator recalls of the past decade, when owners reported warning lights, waiting for parts or worries about their own car. That is a reminder that complaint counts reflect what owners were worried about, not only what broke.

**Driver-assistance systems are a new category.** Forward collision avoidance — automatic emergency braking — collected 17,160 complaints. A common story is "phantom braking": the car brakes hard for an obstacle that isn't there. As these systems became standard, complaints about them moved into the top ten.

## Fires, crashes and injuries

Owners can also flag whether the problem led to a crash, a fire or an injury. Across all reports, **28,458 mention a crash, 10,342 a fire and 25,232 an injury.** These are owner reports, not investigation findings, but they explain why NHTSA reads complaints closely: clusters of similar fire or crash reports are often how a defect investigation starts.

## Problems tend to appear around 50,000 miles

For each model year we record the median mileage owners reported when the problem happened. Across all model years, the typical value is **about 50,000 miles** — roughly when many factory warranties have ended and the first owner sells. If you are buying a car near that mileage, ask what has already been repaired.

## How to use this when you shop

- **Look at the pattern, not just the total.** On every model-year page we list the components owners complain about most. A model whose complaints are spread thinly is very different from one where half the reports are about the transmission.
- **Compare model years of the same model.** Popular cars collect more complaints simply because there are more of them. Our [problems-by-year pages](/honda/cr-v/problems) show which year of a model draws the most reports.
- **Turn the top components into a test-drive plan.** If engines dominate, cold-start the car and listen; if it's the transmission, drive through every gear; if it's electrical, test every switch and screen. Each model-year page includes a printable used-car checklist built this way.
- **Remember the limits.** Complaints are unverified and depend on how many owners bother to report. Read our guide on [how to read complaint counts](/guide/read-complaints-mpg) before drawing conclusions.

*Source: NHTSA owner complaints received from 2015 to October 2026 for the models listed on DecodedCars, grouped by the first component of each report. Figures are updated as new complaints arrive.*
