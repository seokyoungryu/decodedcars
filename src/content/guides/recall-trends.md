DecodedCars tracks **4,526 NHTSA recall campaigns** issued since 2010 that affect the passenger cars, SUVs, pickups and vans on the site. Counting them by the year each recall was filed, and by the part being fixed, shows how the recall system has changed — and why a long recall list is less alarming than it looks.

## Recalls per year

| Year filed | Campaigns |
|---|---|
| 2010 | 138 |
| 2012 | 136 |
| 2014 | 276 |
| 2016 | 284 |
| 2018 | 295 |
| 2020 | 263 |
| 2022 | 318 |
| 2024 | 371 |
| 2025 | 386 |

**2014 was the turning point.** The number of campaigns roughly doubled from 2013 to 2014 and never went back down. That was the year of a major ignition-switch scandal and the rapid expansion of the largest air bag inflator recall in U.S. history, and it was followed by record fines and closer oversight. Since then, manufacturers have tended to recall early and broadly rather than risk penalties.

**The count has kept climbing.** 2025 had the most campaigns of any year we track. Part of the reason is complexity: cars now carry dozens of computers, cameras and sensors, and each one is another thing that can need a fix. Part is the growing number of models, especially electric ones from new manufacturers.

## What gets recalled

The most common systems named in recall campaigns:

| System | Campaigns |
|---|---|
| Air bags | 634 |
| Electrical system | 557 |
| Power train | 319 |
| Fuel system (gasoline) | 275 |
| Seat belts | 228 |
| Hydraulic brakes | 222 |
| Equipment (jacks, accessories, labels) | 209 |
| Exterior lighting | 201 |
| Steering | 201 |
| Seats | 198 |
| Structure | 186 |
| Rearview camera (back-over prevention) | 185 |

**Air bags lead, by a wide margin.** Inflator problems were filed as many separate campaigns, often one per manufacturer and year, so they inflate this count.

**Rearview cameras are a recent addition.** Backup cameras became mandatory on new cars from May 2018. A blank or delayed camera image fails the federal standard, so software glitches that would once have been a nuisance now trigger a recall — often fixed with a dealer software update.

**Many recalls are about paperwork and labels.** "Equipment" campaigns include things like incorrect tire-pressure labels or owner's manuals, which matter for compliance but rarely for day-to-day safety.

## "Do Not Drive" notices are rare

When a defect is severe enough, the manufacturer tells owners to stop driving the car or to park it outside until it is repaired. Of the 4,526 campaigns, **77 carried a "Do Not Drive" notice and 78 a "Park Outside" warning** — together about 3% of all recalls. These are the recalls that should stop a used-car purchase until the VIN shows the repair was done. Every model-year page on DecodedCars flags them in red.

## What this means for buyers

- **A long recall list is normal.** A popular model that has been on sale for several years will collect recalls almost automatically, because multi-model campaigns sweep it in. Read [is a car with many recalls a bad buy?](/guide/are-recalls-a-red-flag) for how to judge them.
- **What matters is whether your car was fixed.** Recall repairs are free, even on used cars. Run the VIN at NHTSA before buying — see [how to check if your car has an open recall](/guide/check-recalls).
- **Look at the type of recall.** A software update for a camera is very different from a fuel leak. The latest campaigns are on our [recalls page](/recalls), and each model-year page lists its recalls with the problem, the risk and the fix.
